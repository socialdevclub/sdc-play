# Party Game Optimization Insights

## Core Game Design Principles Discovered

### 1. The 15-Player Challenge
- **Information Overload**: 105 potential 1:1 interactions impossible to track
- **Attention Management**: Need clear focal points (TV dashboard)
- **Engagement Layers**: Must work for leaders, middle pack, and strugglers simultaneously

### 2. The 45-Minute Constraint
- **Fast Onboarding**: 5 minutes maximum to understand and start
- **Clear Phases**: Mental model of beginning → middle → end
- **Climactic Finale**: Last 5 minutes must be intense

### 3. Offline Social Dynamics
- **Physical Presence**: Leverage face-to-face for bluffing, deals
- **Public vs Private**: TV (public) vs Phone (private) information
- **Social Proof**: Visible actions influence others (herd behavior)

## Key Success Factors

### Immediate Feedback
- Every action must have visible consequence
- Price changes, animations, sounds
- "I did that!" moments

### Strategic Depth Without Complexity
- Simple rules (0.2% impact)
- Emergent strategies (pump & dump, contrarian)
- Multiple viable paths to victory

### Dramatic Moments
- Breaking news events
- Power rankings shifts
- Final countdown tension

### Social Interaction Catalysts
- Forced trading periods
- Information asymmetry
- Reason to talk/negotiate

## Rejected Ideas & Why

### Order Book System
- Too complex for casual party game
- Requires constant attention
- Not fun for spectators

### 1% Impact
- Too volatile with 15 players
- Single player too influential
- Games end too quickly

### Complex Decay Systems
- Hard to understand impact
- Reduces immediacy of feedback
- Over-engineered for party setting

## Technical Learnings

### Current Architecture Strengths
- Polling-based updates work fine (no WebSocket needed)
- Good separation of concerns
- Type safety throughout

### Areas for Improvement
- Mobile UI needs quick-action optimization
- TV dashboard could be more dramatic
- Host controls currently limited

## Cultural Considerations
- Korean party game culture values quick rounds
- Physical proximity enables unique mechanics
- Group dynamics more important than individual skill
